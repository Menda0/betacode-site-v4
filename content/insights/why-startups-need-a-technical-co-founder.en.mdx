Over the years we have met hundreds of founders. Most are not short of ambition or ideas. What they lack is a technical partner who treats the product as their own: someone who makes the architecture calls, challenges bad ideas, ships the MVP and stays when things go wrong.

## The freelancer trap

Freelancers are great for one-off tasks: a landing page, an integration, a bug fix. But they are no substitute for a co-founder. They don’t know your users, don’t worry about your runway and leave when a better project comes along. They deliver output, not accountability.

Agencies are also a poor fit for an early-stage startup. They build what is in the spec, bill monthly and move on. There is no shared risk. And when the spec is wrong, which early on it almost always is, the founder pays and starts over.

## What a technical co-founder actually does

- Shapes the product roadmap with the business founder, rather than just executing tickets.
- Chooses the stack and architecture with scale in mind, without over-engineering early on.
- Ships the MVP and iterates on real feedback, not assumptions.
- Advises on build vs. buy, technical debt and when to pivot.
- Helps hire and mentor the first in-house engineers.
- Tells investors a credible technical story.

## The scarcity problem

Good technical co-founders are hard to find. They are usually building their own projects or have equity tied up elsewhere. Hiring one before there is traction is almost impossible. And giving 30 to 50% of the company to someone you met at a networking event is a bet most founders are not ready to make.

## The gap Betacode Ventures was created to fill

Marco and I built our first startup, Wishmood, with complementary skills: business vision on one side, technical execution on the other. That partnership let us learn fast, pivot and, later, start Betacode.

> Betacode Ventures exists to give other founders the technical partner we had in each other.

## How it works

1. Validate and plan: before writing code, we identify the riskiest assumption, the first users and the minimum useful version.
2. Build and launch: a dedicated full-stack team handles architecture, backend, frontend, infrastructure and deployment.
3. Iterate and scale: we gather feedback, ship improvements and, once the product is proven, help bring talent in-house.

<Note title="The Coach ID case">
The first Betacode Ventures project: a platform built by football coaches for football coaches, rebuilt from scratch after 8 years on the market. In its first week it had more than 100 customers.
</Note>

## Is it right for you?

It is not for every startup. It makes sense for founders with domain knowledge and a clear problem who need technical leadership to validate, build and scale. If you are still exploring a vague idea with no target user, the best start is a conversation, not a partnership.
